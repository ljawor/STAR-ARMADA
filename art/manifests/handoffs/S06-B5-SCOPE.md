# S06 B5 scope — what the environment rows actually are

**Status: resolved from live code, not yet authored. This note is a scope finding, not an approval,
and it asks the owner one question at the end.**

`tools/art/probe_b5_scope.py` prints the table below from the repository, so it can be re-derived
rather than trusted. The B5 batch is written into the roadmap as "environment props", which reads like
fourteen meshes. It is not fourteen meshes: the rows are four different asset classes, and only one of
them fits the pipeline the previous batches used.

| row | what the renderer actually draws | class |
|---|---|---|
| `environment:agricultural`, `barren`, `gas_giant`, `industrial`, `military`, `research`, `temperate`, `trade` | a `planet` shader sampling `surfaceMap` on a shared unit UV sphere (`render/renderer.py::_build_textured_sphere_list`, `_draw_planets`); the map itself is generated by `render/textures.py::planet_texture` with numpy at startup — **no asset file** | 8 × generated texture |
| `environment:star`, `environment:nebula` | billboard textures: `star_texture`, `nebula_texture`, also generated at startup | 2 × generated texture |
| `environment:deep_space_sky_dome`, `environment:jump_point` | effects drawn from primitives (`_draw_starfield`, `_draw_jump_points`): a star dome and a jump glyph, neither of which has surface geometry | 2 × effect |
| `environment:asteroid_field` | `_draw_asteroid_field` draws **40 rocks per field**, sizes 12–46 units, each rock 3–5 lumpy jittered sub-rocks, per instance from a seeded RNG — real triangles, generated, never authored | 1 × geometry |
| `menu:cinematic:backdrop` | a baked movie (`tools/build_menu_movie.py`, which exists at this revision); drawn by `render/menus.py::_draw_splash_movie` | 1 × baked movie |

## The seam situation

`mesh_for('environment:…')` is consulted **nowhere** in `render/` or `game/`: unlike ships, stations,
satellites and planet installations, no environment row has an authored-model seam today. Each class
therefore needs its own answer before an asset can be addressable:

- **the asteroid row** is geometry, and the S01 scope answer already chose the reading for it
  explicitly — asked whether per-instance procedural asteroid props should stay procedural or become a
  shared authored prop, the owner answered *"in scope — bring into the catalogue"*. So a `mesh_for`
  consult in `_draw_asteroid_field` plus a shared authored prop is the authorized implementation, not
  a new decision. It is the one row this batch can author on today's pipeline.
- **the ten texture rows** are a class the pipeline does not have. `art/manifests/pipeline.json`, the
  registry, the loader (`render/authored_mesh.py`: triangles, vertices, bytes) and the export wrappers
  are all mesh-shaped; a texture payload needs its own schema, identity rule, envelope equivalent
  (resolution? bytes?), evidence shape and loader path. The S03 storage policy anticipates exactly this
  and gates it: *"re-examine before the first batch that ships textures"*.
- **the two effect rows** are not surfaces at all: a star dome and a jump glyph have no geometry to
  author, and "bringing them into the catalogue" would mean cataloguing a shader effect, not a model.
- **the backdrop** already has its own pipeline (baked per revision by `tools/build_menu_movie.py`, with
  the menu-cinematic reference covering capture, baking and playback), and the S01 answer left live-vs-
  baked as an implementation choice. It is not a mesh batch item either.

## What this means for B5

The batch splits into one row that today's pipeline can take, ten rows that need a new asset class and
the storage re-examination the policy demands, two rows that are effects, and one row that already has
its own pipeline. Authoring "thirteen environment props" as meshes would mean thirteen assets of the
wrong kind.

**The question for the owner, in one line:** do you want the **texture class opened** (a pipeline step:
schema, loader path, evidence shape and the storage re-examination — which the ten planet/star/nebula
rows need before they can be authored as assets), or should B5 stop at the **asteroid prop** for now
and leave the planet surfaces, the two effects and the baked backdrop on their existing pipelines with
that recorded as the deliberate scope?

Either way the asteroid prop is the first thing to build, and it is the only B5 row that needs no new
class.

## The asteroid row's own constraints, measured

- The prop replaces the *shape* drawn inside `_draw_rock`, not the placement: the field's 40 instances,
  their per-instance scale (12–46 units), tumble and seeded position all stay as they are, so the
  authored rock is a **unit prop** the field scales - built at the size `_draw_rock(1.0)` draws, and
  keeping the RNG variety by being rotated, scaled and tinted per instance as today.
- **The triangle count multiplies by forty.** The legacy rock is 2–4 sub-rock hulls of 24 triangles,
  i.e. 48–96 triangles per instance and roughly 2–4 thousand per field. The exporter refuses a mesh at
  or under 1,800 triangles, so an authored prop starts near a hundred thousand triangles per field drawn
  as 40 display-list calls. That is not disqualifying - a station is 11,500 and a battlefield holds
  dozens - but it is a real cost the prop's detail has to be argued against, and it is why the prop
  should be authored to a stated budget rather than to "as detailed as it looks".
- The row's visual language has to survive being 40 copies at 4–20 units across, seen mostly at tactical
  distance: silhouette irregularity and a lit/dark face split read; per-instance greebles do not.
